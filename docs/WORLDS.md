# Shared worlds: implementation and follow-up

The reading lounge remains available. Midnight Pines adds a bounded 48 × 48 tile forest with eight campfire seats, tree paths, an abandoned cabin, a camper and shared screen. Art is procedural pixel canvas; no external image assets or accounts are required. Each player starts with a flashlight. Marshmallow roasting is available in the emote menu near the fire; walking cancels roasting. Quieter filtered fire crackles, forest wind and occasional synthetic distant calls use the ambience-volume control. A saved local “Mute game sounds” control silences ambience and soundboard effects independently of voice and shared-media volume.

## Authority and transitions

`packages/config` owns map bounds, solids, seats, eight distinct spawns, camera policy, fire location and media-surface geometry. Shared simulation accepts a world map. Colyseus remains the room authority at 60 simulation ticks and 20 snapshots per second. No database migration is required.

Any connected member can suggest a different world from the side menu. The server issues an eight-second proposal, shown to everyone. A matching objection before the deadline cancels it. An unopposed deadline increments `worldRevision`, clears movement intents/seats/roasting and sends every member to deterministic distinct spawns. Duplicate command IDs, stale revisions, simultaneous proposals and late objections are handled by the authority. Suggestions have a ten-second per-person cooldown. No asset-loading barrier is needed for bundled procedural assets.

Members joining during a countdown receive the proposal; members joining afterward receive the current world. A dropped member keeps their place for the existing 30-second reconnect grace and transitions with the group. Actual Colyseus reconnect testing exposed a preexisting client-object identity mismatch; reconnect now checks the reserved session ID, while replaced sessions remain rejected. A page reload retains the local identity; reopening a saved home offers the existing explicit “Use this tab” replacement action when its previous session is still reserved.

World/media state is held in the live room. It resets after server restart or disposal of an empty room. Persistent home access, profiles and the idea board retain their existing SQLite behavior. Garden Dash remains the existing opt-in, per-player activity; a social-world transition gathers racers back into the chosen world. Deciding whether all future game activities must also be room-wide remains a product choice.

## Light, media and sound

Canvas compositing reveals the ground, trees and avatars in the same flickering warm fire field. Directional flashlights reveal nearby paths; distant avatars and their labels/bubbles are hidden outside the visible field. This is atmosphere rather than a security boundary: the authoritative snapshot still carries all room positions. Light does not ray-cast shadows behind obstacles in this milestone.

The lounge and forest both position one shared media surface from the world definition. Its DOM projection now updates on the same render frame as the camera instead of a separate timer. The host pastes a YouTube watch/share/Shorts link or an HTTPS MP4, WebM or Ogg file link and controls play, pause and seek. YouTube uses the official iframe API in the expanded viewing panel (at least 200 × 200 pixels); it pauses locally when the panel closes, while room playback state continues. Provider autoplay/embedding/region restrictions still apply. The server stores a revisioned playback position anchored to its clock; clients converge when drift exceeds 0.6 seconds. Local screen volume is personal, and surface audio fades over 12 tiles. Expanding the viewing panel opts into full listening volume. Playback state survives a world change. Autoplay restrictions require a local tap when a browser blocks playback.

The video origin must permit playback and support byte-range requests for reliable seeking. URLs ending in a supported extension are accepted; this does not guarantee the remote video exists or is playable. Protected streaming websites, uploads and live screen sharing remain unsupported. Browser verification uses an intercepted HTTPS URL with a locally generated test video; it does not validate an external provider or Internet latency.

Native voice is still unavailable: the current media-status endpoint reports it unconfigured and token issuance is disabled. Existing policy controls and proximity gain helpers do not constitute a working voice transport. Completing voice requires an authorized provider configuration, scoped room/member token issuance, client track subscription, positional gain updates, permission/error handling, and testing proximity/whole-room/off modes. No credentials or paid services were created. Text chat, soundboard and forest ambience remain usable with Discord.

## Browser and performance policy

Hidden rendering sleeps independently of sockets and audio. Movement is neutralized on blur/visibility change, prediction and paths reset on transitions, and the server's stale-input timeout remains a fallback. Visual effect queues are bounded at 64 and expired effects are skipped. Foreground React snapshot updates are capped around 5 Hz; live snapshots still drive media and ambience in the background. Rendering resumes from current authoritative state. Background media can continue if the browser permits it; OS/browser audio or frozen-tab restrictions remain outside app control.

Static forest art is cached across transitions; identical trees/logs share textures. Avatar variants are capped at 256 cached textures, pruning unused variants toward 192. Fire/flashlight compositing uses a 320 × 320 mask at up to 30 Hz, with at most eight cones, rather than per-pixel lighting across the full forest. The authored floor is one 1536 × 1536 canvas. These are bounds in code, not measured performance claims.

Suggested profiling acceptance criteria before expanding the map/art:

- Eight moving avatars plus video and voice: target at least 30 FPS on the minimum supported mobile device, 60 FPS on a typical desktop; record frame-time percentiles and long tasks separately.
- Set an initial renderer budget of 8 ms per desktop frame (including at most 3 ms lighting), then revise from measured device traces. Measure media decoding and voice separately.
- Ten world-switch cycles and repeated avatar edits should plateau in canvas/texture count and heap after collection; no increasing listeners/audio nodes.
- One foreground and three background tabs: no hidden render frames or stuck movement; room/video controls remain synchronized and resuming produces no stale-effect burst.
- Profile 30 minutes at eight players on realistic network delay/loss; record input/snapshot bandwidth, drift, reconnect success and authority tick time. Full JSON snapshots and click-path grid construction are observed optimization candidates, not measured bottlenecks. Cache navigation grids or use indexed search/chunked floors only if traces justify it.

## Forest stalker encounter

Each forest room owns at most one clown. Approximately every 25.5–34.5 seconds, it chooses a connected explorer more than 9.5 tiles from the fire and nearby cover (tree, camper or cabin), peeks for three seconds, then pursues for up to fourteen seconds with uneven strides. If no eligible explorer/cover exists, it retries in five seconds. Everyone within ten tiles receives the same encounter; distant clients receive no clown state. It uses the same map collision and path rules, replanning obstructed paths at most once per 1.2 seconds, with movement at ten updates per second. No client timers create enemies.

The nine-tile fire radius is safe. Returning inside it, disconnecting, entering the race, or losing the target causes the clown to retreat. A catch requires proximity and an unobstructed segment; it locks movement for the fade, then returns the target to an unoccupied campfire spawn without inventory or progress loss. A five-second halo fades over its last 1.5 seconds. Respawn counts invalidate old input/prediction paths; the world and media timeline stay intact. A world switch discards the encounter and halo. Original sprite textures are a fixed four-frame set; reduced motion suppresses the sprite's wobble. Giggles, heavy footsteps and a short catch impact now share the positional game-audio bus.

## Campfire calm, proximity cues and death fade

With everyone safely within nine tiles of the fire, hunting is suspended. A separate timer waits 5–10 minutes of continuous campfire safety, then schedules one non-hostile peek from nearby cover just outside the light. It creeps out and returns to cover without converting into a chase if someone subsequently wanders out. Leaving the safe group starts the explorer cadence again. Giggles are server-selected on some appearances and sent as ephemeral events only to nearby listeners.

Catches send one positional knife swish/impact to the victim and listeners within twelve tiles. The victim hears their impact at their personal game volume; others receive quadratic distance falloff. Movement is locked for 900 ms, the page fades to black, then the avatar respawns and the page fades back out over the following 900 ms. The finite CSS overlay aligns to room time, cancels on world/room changes, has no idle render loop, and does not stop background voice or movie playback. The existing halo follows the actual respawn.

Each player's footsteps are very soft, roll off to zero by eight tiles, and respect individual sound muting. Clown boot steps are heavier; their cadence accelerates from 520 to 220 ms as the clown closes on its target, while each listener's volume falls with distance. Samples are short original procedural audio cached once per AudioContext; at most sixteen game cues are active. Hidden/resumed clients schedule at most one current step per source, with no catch-up bursts. All these cues use the game mute/ambience-and-movement volume, independently of voice and screen volume. Synthesized giggle/footstep/knife timbres still benefit from listening and later recorded-asset refinement. Native voice remains unconfigured; sound levels are designed to sit under conversation without claiming a functioning proximity-voice transport.

## Entry, loading and connection health
New room instances start in Midnight Pines; existing live rooms retain the destination chosen by their members. A world request has just the shared eight-second countdown (still cancellable by an objection), with no extra per-member travel cooldown. Commands remain revision checked, deduplicated and rate limited.

Loading indicators cover entry/save requests, renderer import and first rendered frame for each room instance, missing initial snapshots, and file/YouTube buffering. They finish when work completes, without a forced delay. Reduced motion uses a static indicator. Background rendering, voice and media policies remain independent.

An authenticated room ping echoes one bounded numeric nonce, rate limited to one per second; foreground clients probe every two seconds with at most one outstanding request and five retained samples. The indicator reports median round-trip delay, uneven delivery, stale incoming snapshots, offline and reconnecting states. More than 250 ms median is high delay; a 150 ms spread across at least three samples is uneven; two seconds without room updates or two probe timeouts is stalled. Background tabs suspend probing and discard old measurements on resume. These are diagnostic thresholds, not benchmark results or a measurement of Wi-Fi signal strength; server/device lag can also contribute.

## Session recovery
The current room and its short-lived Colyseus reconnection token are retained in sessionStorage for this tab only. Refresh resumes the existing reserved identity, active destination and exact home position while the server grace period remains valid. Explicit Leave clears this record. Network drops keep the game view and try SDK reconnection; if the old runtime ended, a bounded fresh admission restores the same authorized home without automatically replacing another tab. A complete backend restart loses in-memory map/race state and returns the room to a safe Midnight Pines spawn. Existing access checks still apply; offline/online recovery and stale async-connection guards prevent a late join from undoing an explicit leave.

Colyseus close code 4001 denotes server shutdown, and 4003 denotes failed SDK reconnection. Neither proves tab replacement or access revocation. Actual replacement (4011) stops automatic recovery; fresh admission checks access again. Body-only hydration suppression covers browser-extension attributes; descendant hydration checks stay enabled.

Media state is still shared across the room, but playback surfaces exist only indoors. Walking out closes the watching panel and stops local media. ## Watching controls and buffering
The asylum TV keeps one mounted YouTube player when expanded, collapsed or fullscreen. Shared room controls own source, play/pause and seek; volume is personal. Queue entries retain pasted timestamps, and playback IDs prevent duplicate end reports from skipping two entries. Automatic queue advancement requires at least one mounted client player.

YouTube synchronization applies changed playback anchors once (queue-only revisions do not seek). Automatic drift correction is disabled during ordinary playback. Decoder delays, buffering and keyframe rounding cannot cause repeated seeks. Joining or an explicit shared play/pause/seek command aligns the player once. Queue advancement follows the provider ended event, never a comparison of the shared clock to the local duration. Autoplay rejection presents a local enable button and suppresses timer retries. This allows small drift to protect smooth playback; it is not frame-exact synchronization.

Run the opt-in real-provider check with YOUTUBE_LIVE=1 against the local preview; the deterministic adapter test separately injects seven seconds of buffering and tests shared controls, queue, fullscreen and persistent TV playback. Chrome-only live results do not guarantee every stream, browser or connection.

## Flashlight spawn and walk-up charging
Fresh campers and actual camp respawns receive a full, switched-off flashlight, so waiting at spawn does not consume the 30-second enabled budget. Reserved reconnects and ordinary area travel preserve charge and toggle. Both camper and asylum charging docks instantly refill within 0.8 tiles along a walkable approach, without clicking or seating. OFF stays OFF; standing at the dock keeps the battery charged. Disconnected avatars do not recharge.

YouTube player clicks use an app-owned over-video Play/Pause control that sends the same revisioned room commands as Play Together. Provider overlays are noninteractive, preventing local-only playback from fighting paused room authority; fullscreen retains shared controls. Clicking outside Watching Together or pressing Escape closes the panel without pausing the shared stream.
