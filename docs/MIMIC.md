# Forest mimic

One room-owned mimic can disguise itself using a captured human or NPC avatar appearance. It has no roster/chat/session identity and cannot occupy a human seat or room slot. The visual stays stable if the source changes appearance or disconnects.

It checks for a spawn roughly every two minutes and defers if every suitable cover is visible to a human. The server uses conservative 40×28-tile camera bounds plus three tiles of padding, including map-edge camera clamping, for every connected outdoor human. The renderer respects the same maximum dimensions; no client-supplied viewport can weaken spawn safety. A wider snapshot interest radius means the disguised actor approaches into view rather than appearing at a visibility cutoff.

The mimic approaches at 0.65× walking speed, stops within 3.2 tiles for a 2.2-second close linger, roars once, and transforms over 360 ms. Pursuit accelerates to 1.9× walking speed with a bounded heavy stride. A substantially nearer fleeing player can attract it, with hysteresis/cooldown and a fresh 900 ms catch warning. Fire, interiors, races, disconnected and respawn-protected humans remain safe. A retreat reserves the encounter for 15 seconds and cannot reappear at an origin another human is now viewing. Only one clown, wolf or mimic is active, including hidden retreat reservations.

The louder roar uses a maximum source gain of 0.30 with a smooth 1.1-second original sample below 0.65 peak (less than 0.195 full-scale contribution before distance/user attenuation). Game mute and positional falloff remain authoritative for audio presentation. Physical speaker balance is unverified.

The shared catch presentation now has a faster closeup (first surge in 54 ms), then a forceful snatch/fall silhouette and finite blackout. There is no gore or strobe. Reduced motion removes the lunge, jolt and grasp; the short accent stays on the independent game-mute/effects controls. Every initial clown greeting now has one positional giggle, including perimeter encounters; there is no speech synthesis.
